Class 12 Chapter 1: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

1. Galvanic Cells, Standard Electrode Potentials & Nernst Equation

Daniell Cell & Half-Cell Conventions

• Anode (Oxidation): Zn(s) → Zn2+(aq) + 2 e- (Negative terminal)
• Cathode (Reduction): Cu2+(aq) + 2 e- → Cu(s) (Positive terminal)
• EMF of Cell: $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = E^\circ_{right} - E^\circ_{left}$
• External Potential ($E_{ext}$):
- If $E_{ext} < 1.1\text{ V}$: current flows from Cu to Zn.
- If $E_{ext} = 1.1\text{ V}$: no electron or current flow.
- If $E_{ext} > 1.1\text{ V}$: reaction reverses; acts as an electrolytic cell (Zn deposits, Cu dissolves).

Nernst Equation & Thermodynamics

• Nernst Equation (at 298 K):
$$E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \frac{0.0591}{n} \log Q_c = E^\circ_{cell} - \frac{2.303 RT}{nF} \log \frac{[\text{Products}]}{[\text{Reactants}]}$$
• Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$):
$$E^\circ_{cell} = \frac{0.0591}{n} \log K_c \implies \log K_c = \frac{n E^\circ_{cell}}{0.0591}$$
• Gibbs Free Energy:
$$\Delta G^\circ = -n F E^\circ_{cell} = -2.303 RT \log K_c$$
(Spontaneous reaction requires $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ and $E^\circ_{cell} > 0$).

2. Conductance, Cell Constant & Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration

Conductivity Metrics & Units

• Conductance ($G$): $G = 1/R$ (Unit: $\Omega^{-1}$, S or mho)
• Conductivity ($\kappa$): $\kappa = G \cdot \frac{l}{A} = \frac{G^*}{R}$ (Unit: $\text{S cm}^{-1}$ or $\text{S m}^{-1}$)
• Cell Constant ($G^*$): $G^* = l / A$ (Unit: $\text{cm}^{-1}$ or $\text{m}^{-1}$)
• Molar Conductivity ($\Lambda_m$): $\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{M}$ (Unit: $\text{S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$)
• Dilution Effect: Upon dilution, $\kappa$ decreases (fewer ions per unit volume) while $\Lambda_m$ increases for both strong and weak electrolytes.

Debye-Hückel-Onsager & Kohlrausch's Law

• Strong Electrolytes: $\Lambda_m = \Lambda^\circ_m - A \sqrt{c}$ (Linear plot with negative slope $-A$).
• Kohlrausch's Law: $\Lambda^\circ_m(A_x B_y) = x \lambda^\circ_+ + y \lambda^\circ_-$
- For Acetic Acid: $\Lambda^\circ_m(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}) = \Lambda^\circ_m(\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}) + \Lambda^\circ_m(\text{HCl}) - \Lambda^\circ_m(\text{NaCl})$
• Degree of Dissociation ($\alpha$): $\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda^\circ_m}$
• Dissociation Constant ($K_a$): $K_a = \frac{c \alpha^2}{1 - \alpha} = \frac{c (\Lambda_m)^2}{\Lambda^\circ_m (\Lambda^\circ_m - \Lambda_m)}$

3. Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis, Batteries, Fuel Cells & Corrosion

Faraday's Laws

• 1st Law: $w = Z \cdot I \cdot t = \frac{E}{96500} \cdot Q$
• 2nd Law: $\frac{w_1}{w_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2}$ (when same current is passed in series)
• $1\text{ Faraday} = 96,487\text{ C} \approx 96,500\text{ C mol}^{-1}$ (charge on 1 mole of electrons).
• Electrolysis of aqueous NaCl: $H_2$ at cathode, $Cl_2$ at anode (due to $O_2$ overpotential), and NaOH in solution.

Primary & Secondary Batteries

• Dry Cell: Zn anode, graphite cathode; paste of $\text{MnO}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$; voltage 1.5 V.
• Mercury Cell: Constant 1.35 V (no ions in overall reaction: $\text{Zn(Hg)} + \text{HgO} \rightarrow \text{ZnO} + \text{Hg}$).
• Lead Storage Battery (Discharge):
Anode: $\text{Pb} + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_4 + 2e^-$
Cathode: $\text{PbO}_2 + \text{SO}_4^{2-} + 4\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ density drops during discharge.

Fuel Cells & Rusting

• $H_2-O_2$ Fuel Cell: Converts combustion energy directly into electricity. Overall: $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $\eta \approx 70\%$.
• Corrosion of Iron (Rusting):
Anode: $2\text{Fe} \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}^{2+} + 4e^-$
Cathode: $\text{O}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^- \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
Rust: $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$
• Protection: Galvanization (sacrificial Zn coating), cathodic protection (Mg/Zn blocks).
